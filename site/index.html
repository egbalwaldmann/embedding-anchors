Publish the Anchors

A Minimal Interoperability Standard for Text Embedding Spaces, with a Cross-Vendor German Migration Study

Egbal Waldmann (ORCID, LinkedIn) · November 2026 · Preprint

A translated index keeps 88–96% of a full re-embed and is never better than the old model.

Paper (PDF) Code and specification Anchor corpus DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24484736

The idea

A vector index dies with the model that wrote it. If every provider published the vectors of one small public collection of texts, the anchor corpus, anyone could fit a map between two spaces, carry an index to another model and measure what that costs. The standard does not require models to be compatible. It makes compatibility measurable.

The study covers five open multilingual models, two commercial spaces as sources, two German Wikipedia testbeds with human queries (Wiki-de, MIRACL-de) and a private German archive (Archive). For the Archive only aggregate numbers per pair of spaces are shown; no text from it is released.

1. How many anchor pairs

A few thousand pairs carry most of what the orthogonal map delivers; how much that is depends on the collection. Below 1,000 pairs the result depends strongly on the pair. Pick a pair of spaces and compare it with the range of all pairs of open models and with keeping the old model.

2. Every pair of spaces

The pairs differ, most of all on the Archive. The family of the two models matters: between the three XLM-R encoders several points more survive than across families. Each cell is the new model's nDCG@10 with the translated index, in per cent of a full re-embed.

Mode

3. Against keeping the old model

A translated index is not better than the old model. A bridge is compared with staying on the old model per query: the queries are resampled 2,000 times, and a difference whose 95% interval includes zero is called a tie. The quality of a translated index is bounded by the old model, whichever space it is translated into.

Mode

4. A partly migrated index

A migration that replaces vectors in place passes through a state in which translated and re-embedded vectors share one index. Such a mixed index is worse than its composition suggests: a translated vector's similarity to a native query is damped, so a native passage is ranked ahead of a translated one whether or not it is the more relevant. Re-embedding a translated index in place makes retrieval worse before it makes it better.

Show

5. What the anchors predict

Three similarity measures compare the two anchor matrices of a pair of spaces without fitting a map. Within one testbed they correlate with the outcome, but the same similarity value goes with a loss of a few per cent on Wiki-de and of more than a tenth on the Archive. A map that fails on held-out anchors will not retrieve; a map that passes has shown very little. That is why the Adapter Manifest requires a measurement.

Mode

6. Hybrid search with BM25

When the old model is gone, the alternative to a translated index is not an empty result list but lexical search, so BM25 is the bar a bridge has to clear. Hybrid runs fuse its ranking with the dense one by reciprocal rank fusion, with equal weights or with a weight tuned on the other half of the queries. Translated vectors add to lexical search even where they are weaker than it.
